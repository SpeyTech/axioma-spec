## 1. Problem

SHALL-048 v0.3 states that an observation exceeding `AX_MAX_OBS_BYTES` "SHALL be recorded with `completion_state = TRUNCATED`". That text has three defects:

1. **It does not say what is measured.** The output, the record without `obs_hash`, the record as stored, or the framed ledger entry give four different numbers for the same observation.
2. **It mislabels the record.** TRUNCATED already means, under SHALL-040, "the output was cut at `AX_MAX_OUTPUT_BYTES` and `output_size` holds the original length". A record that is too large after escaping is a different condition. Relabelling it TRUNCATED either stores a record that is still too large, or cuts output a second time in a way SHALL-040 does not describe.
3. **It was not enforced.** Admission bounded only the raw output (SHALL-040). An output of up to 64,512 bytes whose escaped form is larger produced a COMPLETE record well over the limit: 72,389 octets for 12,000 LF characters (each escaped to six bytes by SHALL-045), and at most 129,407 octets measured (21,503 LF). The ceiling is set by the 131,072-byte canonical buffer, beyond which finding B-01 (output loss) applied instead. Finding SHALL-048 in the Stage 1 report reproduces this on `axioma-oracle` `57ec02b`.

## 2. Amended requirement

**SRS-004-SHALL-048 (as amended by SRS-004-AMD-001)**

*Maximum observation size, enforced by rejection.*

```c
#define AX_MAX_OBS_BYTES  65536  /* 64 KiB; value unchanged */
```

(a) **Measured quantity.** The size of an `AX:OBS:v1` record is the number of octets in its canonical encoding as stored: the UTF-8 byte sequence produced by SHALL-006 and SHALL-045, with all members present and `obs_hash` holding its 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters. The count excludes any string terminator, ledger framing (tag, length prefix, commitment), storage envelope and transport encoding.

(b) **Limit.** A record whose size is at most `AX_MAX_OBS_BYTES` (65,536 octets) is within the limit. 65,536 is admissible; 65,537 is not.

(c) **Point of enforcement.** Admission SHALL compute the size of the complete record, after output normalisation (SHALL-043), any SHALL-040 truncation and `obs_hash` computation (SHALL-046). It SHALL do so before the record is committed and before any output is released to a caller.

(d) **Rejection.** A record over the limit SHALL NOT be committed, stored or served, in whole or in part. Admission SHALL:
   1. fail with a size error (reference implementation: `AX_ERR_SIZE` with `faults->size` set);
   2. produce no `obs_hash`;
   3. leave the admission sequence unchanged.

(e) **No truncation or relabelling.** The record SHALL NOT be truncated, shortened, re-encoded, or relabelled `TRUNCATED` in order to fit. `TRUNCATED` keeps exactly its SHALL-040 meaning.

(f) **Recorded fact of rejection.** A producer that commits observations, such as the gateway, SHALL record the rejection in place of the oversized record, as an `AX:OBS:v1` record with:
   - `completion_state = ERROR`;
   - `failure_type = INVALID_OUTPUT`;
   - empty `output`;
   - `output_size = 0`.

   This record is subject to policy evaluation like any other ERROR observation (SHALL-044) and MAY be treated as `BREACH` by policy. Under v1 it cannot carry the rejected record's size; see §6.

(g) **Relation to SHALL-040.**
   - SHALL-040 bounds the raw output at `AX_MAX_OUTPUT_BYTES`, and still truncates with `completion_state = TRUNCATED` and `output_size` = original length.
   - SHALL-048 then bounds the complete record. A TRUNCATED record that exceeds the limit after escaping is rejected under (d) like any other.

**Verification:** boundary test, property test, inspection (§5).

## 3. Rationale

- Rejection keeps the evidence statement simple: every committed `AX:OBS:v1` record is complete with respect to what it claims, and is never larger than 64 KiB.
- Truncating again after escaping would create a second, undocumented truncation rule. The record would then commit to bytes that were neither what the oracle returned nor what SHALL-040 describes.
- The reject-and-record pattern already exists for non-admissible output (non-NFC, invalid encoding). This amendment reuses it.

## 4. Historical compatibility

1. **No byte change for any record within the limit.** Admission, canonicalisation and hashing of records of 65,536 octets or fewer are unchanged. Stage 1 corpus result: all 302 records that were admitted, complete, within 64 KiB and within identifier bounds on `57ec02b` are byte-identical after the change.
2. **Records already produced over the limit are not rewritten.** Producers before enforcement (`axioma-oracle` `57ec02b` and earlier) could commit COMPLETE records from 65,537 octets up to the canonical-buffer ceiling (129,407 octets measured with LF-only output).
   - Such a record's `obs_hash` may still verify, and its chain position is unaffected.
   - It is classified `V1_OVER_SIZE_LIMIT` by the read-only classifier `tools/obs_v1_loss_check.py`: an integrity-intact v1 record that does not conform to SHALL-048. It SHALL NOT be reported as conformant, truncated or repaired.
3. **Validators.** `ax_obs_validate` checks schema, encoding, size consistency and `obs_hash`; on the Stage 1 branch it does not check record size.
   - **Open decision for the owner (§8, item 2):** whether v1 validators also report over-size as a distinct, non-fatal finding, or whether that stays with the historical classifier.
   - Neither option changes v1 bytes.
4. **Version.** `AX:OBS:v1` is retained. Under the old text, no conformant producer could commit a record over the limit as COMPLETE. The amendment states how the limit is enforced; it does not change the bytes of any admissible record.

Fail-before / pass-after:
- **Cases 1, 2, 4 and 5** fail on `57ec02b` and on the first PCR-001 commit `c4b7aec`, and pass on the Stage 1 head. Case 4 fails there because 11,000 LF is admitted over the limit.
- **Case 3** passes on all three; it guards that SHALL-040 truncation is unchanged.
- Totals: 1 of 5 before, 5 of 5 after.

## 6. Consequences for `AX:OBS:v2` (PCR-005; informative)

- Under v2 escaping (RFC 8785), LF costs 2 octets instead of 6, so the admissible LF count rises from 10,857 to about 32,000 under the same 65,536-octet limit.
- v2 should allow the rejection record to carry the size of the rejected record, for example a `rejected_record_size` member, which v1 cannot express without a schema change.
